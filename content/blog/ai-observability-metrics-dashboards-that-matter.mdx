# AI Observability: Metrics and Dashboards That Matter

Traditional observability focuses on the "Golden Signals": latency, traffic, errors, and saturation. While these remain critical for [system SLOs](/blog/ai-system-slos-non-deterministic-systems), they are insufficient for AI systems. You need a dashboard that also tracks model-specific behavior, such as [inference latency bottlenecks](/blog/why-your-llm-responses-are-slow-diagnosing-inference-latency-production) and [KV cache utilization](/blog/serving-open-source-llms-with-vllm-kubernetes).

## The Three Layers of AI Observability

### Layer 1: Infrastructure Health
This is the baseline. Use **Prometheus and Grafana** to track GPU temperature, VRAM utilization, and [autoscaling events](/blog/gpu-autoscaling-right-sizing-inference-clusters). This visibility is critical for diagnosing issues like [GPU pod OOMKilled events](/blog/kubernetes-oomkilled-gpu-pods-causes-debugging-prevention). If your [incident response runbook](/blog/ai-incident-response-runbooks-production-models) starts with "is the pod alive?", this is where you build that visibility.

### Layer 2: Model Performance
Track Time to First Token (TTFT), tokens per second, and request queue depth. These metrics tell you whether your [batching strategy](/blog/batching-strategies-llm-inference-throughput-vs-latency) is actually working or just increasing wait times.

### Layer 3: Model Quality Proxies
Since ground-truth accuracy is rarely available in real-time, monitor proxies like prediction distribution shifts, [feature drift](/blog/feature-store-reliability-stale-features-break-predictions), and [PII redaction rates](/blog/data-privacy-rag-systems-pii-filtering-before-model).

## Final Takeaway

AI observability is not about having more charts; it's about having the right charts to support fast triage. By connecting infrastructure health with model quality proxies, you ensure that your team can identify and fix silent failures before they impact users.
