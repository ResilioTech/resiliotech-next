# GPU Autoscaling: Right-Sizing Inference Clusters

Traditional [Horizontal Pod Autoscaling (HPA)](/blog/mlops-pipeline-kubernetes-guide) based on CPU or memory is often ineffective for AI workloads. A GPU can be at 100% utilization while your [inference queue explodes](/blog/why-your-llm-responses-are-slow-diagnosing-inference-latency-production) due to [batching misconfiguration](/blog/batching-strategies-llm-inference-throughput-vs-latency).

To scale correctly, you must use **inference-specific metrics**.

## Scaling on the Right Metrics

We recommend scaling based on:
1. **Inference Queue Depth**: The number of requests waiting for a GPU.
2. **Duty Cycle**: The percentage of time the GPU is actively processing.
3. **Concurrency**: The number of active [KV cache sequences](/blog/serving-open-source-llms-with-vllm-kubernetes).

## Managing Scale-Up and Scale-Down

GPU cold starts are expensive (often 60s+). Your autoscaling policy should include:
- **Aggressive Scale-Up**: Start new nodes early to absorb [predicted burst traffic](/blog/ecommerce-ai-infrastructure-recommendation-engines-search-personalization-scale).
- **Graceful Scale-Down**: Use long cooldown periods to avoid "thrashing" and to maintain [warm capacity](/blog/ai-infrastructure-cost-optimization) during minor fluctuations.

## Final Takeaway

GPU autoscaling is a balance between [cost and performance](/blog/capacity-planning-llm-inference-gpu-memory-throughput-slas). By scaling on inference-specific metrics and maintaining a warm buffer, you can ensure that your platform meets demand while minimizing wasted spend.
