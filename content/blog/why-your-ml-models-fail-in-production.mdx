# Why Your ML Models Fail in Production (And How to Fix It)

Most ML models that perform well in a notebook environment fail within weeks of hitting production. These aren't usually model failures; they are **system failures** caused by [infrastructure gaps](/blog/mlops-pipeline-kubernetes-guide), [stale features](/blog/feature-store-reliability-stale-features-break-predictions), and a complete [lack of observability](/blog/ai-observability-metrics-dashboards-that-matter).

## The Top Three Failure Modes

### 1. Data and Concept Drift
The world changes, but your model remains static. Without [continuous monitoring](/blog/setting-up-ml-model-monitoring-prometheus-grafana-complete-guide), you won't know that your model's accuracy is plummeting until your users start complaining.

### 2. Infrastructure Bottlenecks
A Jupyter notebook doesn't have to worry about [GPU autoscaling](/blog/gpu-autoscaling-right-sizing-inference-clusters) or [KV cache pressure](/blog/why-your-llm-responses-are-slow-diagnosing-inference-latency-production). In production, these system constraints directly dictate your [reliability SLOs](/blog/ai-system-slos-non-deterministic-systems).

### 3. Lack of Automated Testing
If you aren't running [automated evals](/blog/building-eval-pipeline-catches-regressions-before-users) and [canary releases](/blog/model-canary-releases-shadow-traffic-guide), you are flying blind.

## Final Takeaway

Production ML is a software engineering problem, not just a data science problem. By building robust MLOps pipelines and deep observability into your platform, you can bridge the gap between notebook experiments and reliable production AI.
