# Vector Database Operations: Scaling, Backup, and Disaster Recovery

In a [production RAG system](/blog/production-rag-systems-reliability-checklist), the vector database (Milvus, Pinecone, Weaviate, or Qdrant) is your source of truth. If your index is slow or unavailable, your [model's accuracy and latency](/blog/why-your-llm-responses-are-slow-diagnosing-inference-latency-production) will suffer immediately.

## Scaling the Vector Index

### 1. Memory vs. Disk Tradeoffs
Vector search is memory-intensive. You must [plan your capacity](/blog/capacity-planning-llm-inference-gpu-memory-throughput-slas) to ensure that your "hot" index fits in RAM. As your data grows, use sharding or disk-based indexing (with NVMe) to maintain performance.

### 2. Backup and Forensic Replay
A database backup is not enough. You must be able to [reconstruct a decision](/blog/ai-audit-logs-regulators-ask-for-how-to-prepare) based on the exact state of the index at the time of the request. This requires versioned indices and audit-ready ingest pipelines.

### 3. Multi-Region High Availability
For [mission-critical applications](/blog/multi-region-ai-disaster-recovery-failover-playbook), your vector store must be replicated across regions to protect against provider or regional outages.

## Final Takeaway

Vector database operations are the "Data" in Data-Centric AI. By building a scalable, backed-up, and highly available vector store, you ensure that your RAG systems remain reliable and trustworthy at any scale.
