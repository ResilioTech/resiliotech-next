# Terraform for AI Infrastructure: GPU Nodes, Model Registries, and Pipelines

Manual infrastructure management is a recipe for [production failures](/blog/why-your-ml-models-fail-in-production). To build a scalable and [reproducible ML platform](/blog/building-internal-ml-platform-kubernetes-what-actually-works), you must treat your [GPU node pools](/blog/gpu-autoscaling-right-sizing-inference-clusters) and networking as versioned code.

**Terraform** is the industry standard for this task.

## What to Codify in Terraform

### 1. GPU Node Pools and Autoscaling
Define your [capacity planning](/blog/capacity-planning-llm-inference-gpu-memory-throughput-slas) requirements in HCL. This allows you to spin up new regions or [disaster recovery sites](/blog/multi-region-ai-disaster-recovery-failover-playbook) in minutes rather than days.

### 2. Private Model Registries and Storage
Codify the [secrets and access controls](/blog/secrets-management-ai-pipelines-api-keys-model-weights-credentials) for your model artifacts. This ensures that only authorized CI/CD pipelines can push or pull production weights.

### 3. Network and Security Boundaries
Use Terraform to provision the [VPCs, Subnets, and NetworkPolicies](/blog/ai-infrastructure-fintech-latency-compliance-real-time-fraud-detection) required for your private AI environment.

## Final Takeaway

Infrastructure-as-Code is the "Ops" in MLOps. By codifying your AI platform in Terraform, you ensure that your environment is predictable, audit-ready, and easily scalable as your model count grows.
