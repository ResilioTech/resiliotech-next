# Building an MLOps Pipeline on Kubernetes: A Practical Guide

A production MLOps pipeline is what separates a data science experiment from a reliable software feature. On Kubernetes, this means [moving from notebook-based ML](/blog/moving-from-notebook-based-ml-to-production-pipelines-practical-path) to a path that automates everything from [data validation](/blog/ci-cd-ml-models-testing-beyond-unit-tests) to [model serving](/blog/serving-open-source-llms-with-vllm-kubernetes). Understanding your [MLOps maturity](/blog/mlops-maturity-model-where-is-your-team-and-what-should-you-build-next) is the first step in this journey.

## The Pillars of K8s MLOps

### 1. Automated Training and Retraining
Use **Kubeflow Pipelines** or **Argo Workflows** to automate your training runs. If you're currently relying on simple scripts, see our guide on [replacing cron jobs with proper ML pipeline orchestration](/blog/replacing-cron-jobs-with-proper-ml-pipeline-orchestration). These should be triggered by [feature drift alerts](/blog/feature-store-reliability-stale-features-break-predictions) or a regular schedule.

### 2. Model Registry and Artifact Management
Store your models in a central registry. Use [Terraform](/blog/terraform-for-ai-infrastructure-gpu-nodes-model-registries-pipelines) to manage the storage and networking required for these artifacts.

### 3. Reliable Serving and Rollouts
Deploy your models using [canary releases or shadow traffic](/blog/model-canary-releases-shadow-traffic-guide). This ensures that new models are validated against production traffic before they impact your [system SLOs](/blog/ai-system-slos-non-deterministic-systems).

## Final Takeaway

An MLOps pipeline on Kubernetes provides the automation and consistency needed for production AI. By standardizing your release path and integrating [deep observability](/blog/ai-observability-metrics-dashboards-that-matter), you enable your team to ship better models, faster.
