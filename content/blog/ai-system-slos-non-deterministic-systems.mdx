# AI System SLOs: Defining Uptime for Non-Deterministic Systems

In traditional software, a "99.9% uptime" SLO is unambiguous. In AI, a system can be "up" (returning 200 OK) while being completely broken (serving hallucinations or stale predictions). This requires a shift from binary uptime to [multi-layered observability](/blog/ai-observability-metrics-dashboards-that-matter).

## Defining Meaningful AI SLIs

To build robust SLOs, you must first define your Service Level Indicators (SLIs). For [production LLMs](/blog/serving-open-source-llms-with-vllm-kubernetes), this usually includes:

- **TTFT (Time to First Token)**: < 200ms for interactive chat.
- **Inference Latency**: < 2s for [real-time fraud detection](/blog/ai-infrastructure-fintech-latency-compliance-real-time-fraud-detection).
- **Quality Proxy**: > 95% of responses must pass [automated eval filters](/blog/building-eval-pipeline-catches-regressions-before-users).

## Managing the Error Budget

Your error budget isn't just for downtime; it's for experiments, [canary releases](/blog/model-canary-releases-shadow-traffic-guide), and [A/B testing](/blog/ab-testing-llm-outputs-statistical-methods-non-numeric-responses). If a new model version degrades your quality SLI, it should consume the error budget just like a traditional outage would.

## Final Takeaway

SLOs for AI systems must bridge the gap between infrastructure availability and model correctness. By defining clear SLIs for latency and quality, you provide your engineering and product teams with a common language for reliability and risk.
