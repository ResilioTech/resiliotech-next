# Multi-Region AI Disaster Recovery: Failover Playbook

For [mission-critical AI systems](/blog/deploying-ai-healthcare-hipaa-compliant-ml-infrastructure-kubernetes), a single-region outage is a catastrophic risk. To maintain [high availability](/blog/ai-system-slos-non-deterministic-systems), you need a multi-region disaster recovery (DR) playbook that covers both your [inference fleet](/blog/serving-open-source-llms-with-vllm-kubernetes) and your [data store](/blog/vector-database-operations-scaling-backup-disaster-recovery).

## The Multi-Region AI Architecture

### 1. Active-Active Inference
Run your [LLM gateways](/blog/llm-gateway-architecture-routing-rate-limits-cost-controls) and inference services in multiple regions simultaneously. If one region's [GPU capacity explodes](/blog/why-your-llm-responses-are-slow-diagnosing-inference-latency-production), the gateway should automatically shift traffic.

### 2. Data and Model Replication
Replicate your [model registry](/blog/terraform-for-ai-infrastructure-gpu-nodes-model-registries-pipelines) and [feature store](/blog/feature-store-reliability-stale-features-break-predictions) asynchronously across regions. Ensure that your vector indices are also backed up and ready for failover.

### 3. Automated Failover Testing
DR is only as good as your last test. Use **Chaos Engineering** to simulate regional outages and verify that your system recovers within your RTO (Recovery Time Objective).

## Final Takeaway

Multi-region AI is an investment in business continuity. By building a redundant, geographically distributed architecture, you protect your organization from the infrastructure and network failures that inevitably occur at scale.
